# Methodology and limitations

This portfolio edition converts an earlier university study into a decision-oriented case study.
It combines a literature review, official policy and legal sources, service scenarios, weighted
prioritisation, reference architecture, governance analysis, and a qualitative risk register.

## Assumptions

- The subject is a hypothetical medium-sized Greek municipality.
- No municipal interviews, resident survey, production telemetry, or procurement data were available.
- Scores use an explicit five-point scale and represent design judgement.
- Cost, duration, and benefit figures require validation during discovery.
- The architecture identifies capabilities and boundaries rather than selecting vendors.

## Interpretation

The ranking is a transparent hypothesis. It shows why low-dependency services are suitable pilots
and why certificate and payment services should follow stronger integration and control foundations.
It does not claim that every municipality should implement the same sequence.

The legal and regulatory discussion identifies design obligations and review questions. It does not
provide a legal opinion on national transposition, sector scope, or a specific processing operation.
